Chapter 1: Visa Options for Digital Nomads

As of 2026, Egypt does not yet offer a dedicated digital nomad visa. However, the tourist visa system provides flexible pathways for remote workers to base themselves in Egypt for weeks or even months. Here are the three main options digital nomads use.

Tourist e-Visa

Cost: $25 USD single entry. Validity: 90 days from issue, 30-day stay per entry. Extension: can be extended at the Passport and Immigration Office in Cairo for up to 6 months total. Best for: digital nomads wanting a simple, affordable entry with extension option.

Tourist Visa on Arrival

Cost: $25 USD cash at airport. Validity: 30-day stay. Extension: can be extended in Cairo. Best for: digital nomads who prefer to decide on arrival or who need flexibility with timing.

Multiple-Entry Tourist Visa

Cost: $60 USD. Validity: 90 days from issue, 30-day stays per entry. Best for: digital nomads planning visa runs to Jordan, Turkey or other neighboring countries to reset their stay.

Long-Term Stay Strategy for Digital Nomads

Many long-term digital nomads in Egypt combine tourist visas with visa runs to neighboring countries. Common visa run destinations include Jordan (Aqaba), Turkey, and Jordan again. Each visa run resets your 30-day tourist stay. Many digital nomads stay in Egypt for 3-6 months using this strategy. Chapter 3: Best Cities for Digital Nomads

Egypt offers diverse cities with different strengths for digital nomads. Here's a detailed comparison of the top destinations.

Cairo — The Capital Hub

Best for: infrastructure, international community, coworking spaces, airport access, diverse dining. Best neighborhoods: Zamalek (island neighborhood, expat hub), Maadi (quiet, green, expat-friendly), Zamalek (central, vibrant). Internet: 50–200 Mbps fiber. Cost: $800–1,500/month.

Dahab — The Beach Town

Best for: beach lifestyle, Red Sea diving, strong digital nomad community, low cost. Best neighborhoods: Mashraba (expat hub), Assalah (local town). Internet: 30–100 Mbps. Cost: $500–900/month. Best for: long-term stays, diving enthusiasts.

Luxor — The Ancient City

Best for: ancient wonders, low cost, growing expat community, hot air ballooning. Best neighborhoods: East Bank (modern amenities), West Bank (ancient sites). Internet: 30–100 Mbps. Cost: $500–900/month.

Alexandria — The Mediterranean

Best for: Mediterranean lifestyle, lower costs than Cairo, good infrastructure, coastal charm. Best neighborhoods: Anfushi, Stanley, Montaza. Internet: 40–150 Mbps. Cost: $600–1,200/month.

Internet Tips for Digital Nomads

  • Always have backup: carry a mobile data SIM as backup for critical work calls. Vodafone offers the best coverage nationwide.
  • Fiber vs. 4G: fiber is faster and more stable in cities; 4G is reliable but can fluctuate.
  • Coworking spaces: offer the most reliable internet with backup systems; ideal for critical work calls.
  • Power outages: occasional in Egypt; coworking spaces and cafes usually have generators.
  • VPN: recommended for security and accessing some services; most coworking spaces support VPN use.

Chapter 5: Coworking Spaces

Egypt's coworking scene has grown significantly, with spaces in major cities offering reliable internet, community and professional environments for remote workers.

Cairo Coworking Spaces

Top spaces: GrEEK Campus (multiple locations), The District (New Cairo), Consoleya (Zamalek), The Hub (Maadi). Cost: $80–150/month. Features: high-speed internet, meeting rooms, community events, coffee, printing.

Dahab Coworking Spaces

Top spaces: The Bunker, Dahab Lagoon, Co-Working Dahab. Cost: $50–100/month. Features: beach views, diving community, relaxed atmosphere, good internet.

Luxor Coworking Spaces

Top spaces: Luxor Co-Working Space, The Hub Luxor. Cost: $50–100/month. Features: ancient city views, growing expat community, good internet.

Chapter 6: Tax Considerations

Tax obligations for digital nomads in Egypt depend on your home country's tax laws and how long you stay in Egypt. Here's what you need to know.

Tax Residency Rules

Egypt tax residency: if you stay in Egypt less than 183 days per year, you are generally not considered a tax resident of Egypt. Home country obligations: you must still comply with your home country's tax obligations regardless of where you live. Double taxation: Egypt has tax treaties with many countries to prevent double taxation. Consult a tax advisor: consult a tax advisor familiar with both your home country and Egyptian tax laws for personalized guidance.

Important Tax Considerations

Consult a professional: tax laws are complex and vary by individual circumstances. Consult a qualified tax advisor familiar with both your home country and Egyptian tax laws. Documentation: keep detailed records of your time in Egypt and income sources. Visa runs: be aware that frequent visa runs may affect your tax residency status in your home country. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Egypt have a digital nomad visa in 2026?
As of 2026, Egypt does not yet have a dedicated digital nomad visa. However, most digital nomads work remotely from Egypt using the standard tourist visa, which allows stays of up to 30 days per entry and can be extended at the Passport and Immigration Office in Cairo for up to 6 months total. Many digital nomads also use visa runs to neighboring countries (Jordan, Turkey) to reset their tourist visa.
What visa options do digital nomads use to work remotely from Egypt?
Most digital nomads use one of three options: 1) Tourist e-Visa ($25 single entry, 30-day stay, extendable in Cairo), 2) Tourist visa on arrival ($25 USD cash at airport, 30-day stay), or 3) Multiple-entry tourist visa ($60, 90-day validity with 30-day stays per entry). Many long-term digital nomads combine tourist visas with visa runs to Jordan or Turkey every 30-90 days.
What is the cost of living for digital nomads in Egypt?
Egypt is one of the most affordable digital nomad destinations in the world. Monthly costs: Cairo (Zamalek/Maadi) $600-1,200, Dahab $400-800, Luxor $500-900. This includes rent ($200-600), food ($150-300), coworking ($50-150), transport ($50-150), and entertainment ($50-150). A comfortable lifestyle is possible on $800-1,500 per month depending on lifestyle.
Which Egyptian cities are best for digital nomads?
Top cities for digital nomads: 1) Cairo (Zamalek, Maadi) — best infrastructure, coworking spaces, international community, and airport access. 2) Dahab — beach town with strong digital nomad community, excellent internet, affordable cost of living, and Red Sea diving. 3) Luxor — ancient city atmosphere, low cost of living, good internet, growing expat community. 4) Alexandria — Mediterranean coastal city with good infrastructure and lower costs than Cairo.
How is the internet speed in Egypt for remote work?
Internet speeds in Egypt are generally good for remote work. Fiber optic connections in major cities offer 50-200 Mbps. Cairo, Dahab and major coworking spaces typically offer 30-100 Mbps. Mobile data (4G) offers 20-50 Mbps in cities. Recommended providers: Vodafone (best coverage), Orange (good speeds), WE (affordable). Always have a backup mobile data plan for critical work calls.
Do digital nomads need to pay taxes in Egypt?
Tax obligations depend on your home country's tax laws and how long you stay in Egypt. Generally, if you stay in Egypt less than 183 days per year, you are not considered a tax resident of Egypt. However, you must still comply with your home country's tax obligations. Consult a tax advisor familiar with both your home country and Egyptian tax laws.
