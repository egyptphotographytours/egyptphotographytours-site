Chapter 1: West Bank Historical Context — The Land of the Dead

The West Bank of Luxor — ancient Thebes — was the sacred burial ground of pharaohs, queens, and nobles for over 500 years during Egypt's New Kingdom (1550–1070 BC). Ancient Egyptians believed the west bank, where the sun set, was the realm of the dead, while the east bank was the land of the living. This sacred geography shaped one of the world's most extraordinary archaeological landscapes.

The Sacred Geography

Land of the Dead: The West Bank was chosen as the burial ground because the sun set in the west — the direction of death and the afterlife. Pharaohs built their mortuary temples on the West Bank to prepare for their journey into the afterlife.

Sacred Landscape: The Theban hills form a natural pyramid-like peak (al-Qurn), naturally shaped like a pyramid — the pharaohs chose this location for its sacred symbolism.

The Royal Burial Ground

63 Royal Tombs: The Valley of the Kings contains 63 royal tombs from the New Kingdom (1550–1070 BC), including Tutankhamun, Ramses II, Ramses VI, and Seti I.

Hidden Tombs: The pharaohs chose hidden valley locations to protect their tombs from tomb robbers — though most tombs were still robbed in antiquity, Tutankhamun's tomb remained hidden until 1922.

Photography Paradise

Dramatic Landscape: The Theban hills provide a dramatic desert backdrop at sunrise and sunset — ideal for golden hour photography.

Vivid Tomb Colors: Tomb interiors preserve vivid colors after 3,000 years — ideal for interior photography with available light.

Chapter 2: Valley of the Kings — The Royal Burial Ground

The Valley of the Kings is Egypt's most iconic archaeological site — home to 63 royal tombs from Egypt's New Kingdom (1550–1070 BC), including the world-famous tomb of Tutankhamun (KV62). This sacred valley is one of the world's most extraordinary archaeological landscapes.

Chapter 3: Valley of the Queens — The Queens' Burial Ground

The Valley of the Queens is the burial ground of Egypt's queens and royal children during the New Kingdom. Home to over 90 tombs, it includes the world-famous tomb of Queen Nefertari — the most beautiful tomb in Egypt and one of the best-preserved ancient Egyptian tombs.

Queen Nefertari's Tomb (QV66)

Most Beautiful Tomb: The tomb of Queen Nefertari, wife of Ramses II, is the most beautiful tomb in Egypt — featuring vivid colors, well-preserved wall scenes, and spectacular Book of the Dead scenes.

Extra Fee: 1,400 EGP extra — but worth every penny for the most beautiful ancient Egyptian tomb.

Valley of the Queens Highlights

90 Royal Tombs: Home to over 90 tombs of queens and royal children from the New Kingdom.

Less Crowded: Fewer visitors than Valley of the Kings, allowing more peaceful photography and exploration.

Photography Paradise

Vivid Colors: Tomb interiors preserve vivid colors after 3,000 years — ideal for interior photography.

Less Crowded: Fewer visitors than Valley of the Kings, allowing more peaceful photography.

Chapter 4: Valley of the Nobles — Daily Life in Ancient Egypt

The Valley of the Nobles contains over 400 tombs of high-ranking officials and nobles from ancient Egypt. Unlike the royal tombs that focus on the afterlife, the nobles' tombs feature vivid scenes of daily life in ancient Egypt — farming, fishing, hunting, music, and daily life — offering extraordinary insight into daily life in ancient Egypt.

Daily Life Scenes

Daily Life: Unlike royal tombs focused on the afterlife, nobles' tombs feature vivid scenes of daily life — farming, fishing, hunting, music, and daily life in ancient Egypt.

400+ Tombs: Over 400 tombs of high-ranking officials offer extraordinary insight into daily life in ancient Egypt.

Vivid Colors

Vivid Colors: Tomb interiors preserve vivid colors after 3,000 years — ideal for interior photography with available light.

Less Crowded: Fewer visitors than Valley of the Kings, allowing more peaceful photography.

Must-Visit Nobles' Tombs

Tomb of Nakht (TT52): Scribe and astronomer, famous for music and music scenes.

Tomb of Rekhmire (TT100): Vizier of Thutmose III, famous for foreign tribute scenes.

Chapter 5: Hatshepsut Temple at Deir el-Bahari

Hatshepsut Temple at Deir el-Bahari is Egypt's most photogenic temple — a stunning mortuary temple built by Egypt's most powerful female pharaoh, Queen Hatshepsut. Built into the dramatic cliffs of Deir el-Bahari, this terraced temple is one of Egypt's most spectacular archaeological sites.

Hatshepsut Temple at Deir el-Bahari

Egypt's Most Photogenic Temple: Built into the dramatic cliffs of Deir el-Bahari, this terraced mortuary temple is one of Egypt's most spectacular archaeological sites — and one of the most photogenic temples in Egypt.

Hatshepsut's Legacy: Built by Egypt's most powerful female pharaoh, Queen Hatshepsut (1479–1458 BC), one of ancient Egypt's most powerful rulers. The temple is dedicated to the goddess Hathor and the god Amun.

Sunrise Photography: Sunrise shots of Hatshepsut Temple with the dramatic cliff backdrop are spectacular — our tours start at 6:30 AM for sunrise photography.

Chapter 6: Ramesseum & Medinet Habu — Mortuary Temples

The Ramesseum and Medinet Habu are two of Egypt's best-preserved mortuary temples, offering extraordinary photography opportunities and fewer crowds than the more famous temples.

Ramesseum (Mortuary Temple of Ramses II)

Ramses II's Mortuary Temple: Built by Ramses II (1279–1213 BC), one of ancient Egypt's most powerful pharaohs. Features colossal statues of Ramses II and spectacular battle scenes.

Less Crowded: Fewer visitors than Valley of the Kings, allowing more peaceful photography and exploration.

Medinet Habu (Mortuary Temple of Ramses III)

Ramses III's Mortuary Temple: One of Egypt's best-preserved mortuary temples, featuring spectacular battle scenes and well-preserved wall scenes.

Less Crowded: Fewer visitors than Valley of the Kings, allowing more peaceful photography.

Photography Paradise

Spectacular Battle Scenes: Both temples feature spectacular battle scenes from the reigns of Ramses II and Ramses III.

Less Crowded: Fewer visitors than Valley of the Kings, allowing more peaceful photography.

Chapter 7: Colossi of Memnon — Colossal Statues

The Colossi of Memnon are two colossal stone statues of Pharaoh Amenhotep III that have stood for over 3,400 years. These colossal statues are one of Egypt's most iconic archaeological sites — and offer spectacular sunrise photography opportunities.

Colossi of Memnon

3,400-Year-Old Statues: Two colossal stone statues of Pharaoh Amenhotep III (1386–1353 BC) that have stood for over 3,400 years.

Sunrise Photography: Sunrise shots of the Colossi of Memnon are spectacular — our tours start at 6:30 AM for sunrise photography at the Colossi.

Free Entry: No entry fee — the Colossi of Memnon are free to visit.

Photography Tips for West Bank

  • Start Early: The Valley of the Kings opens at 7:00 AM — arrive early for sunrise photography and before crowds arrive.
  • Flash Prohibited: Flash photography is prohibited inside all tombs — use available light and high ISO settings for interior photography.
  • Photographic Permit: Required for photography inside tombs (300 EGP extra).
  • Sunrise Photography: Sunrise shots of Hatshepsut Temple, Colossi of Memnon, and Valley of the Kings exterior are spectacular — our tours start at 6:30 AM for sunrise photography.
  • Interior Tomb Photography: Use available light and high ISO settings (ISO 1600–3200) for interior tomb photography. Wide-angle lens (16–35mm) recommended for tomb interiors.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which tombs in the Valley of the Kings should I visit?
The standard Valley of the Kings ticket covers 3 tombs. Must-visit tombs include: 1) Tomb of Ramses VI (KV9) — spectacular astronomical ceiling and well-preserved wall scenes; 2) Tomb of Ramses IV (KV2) — vivid colors and well-preserved ceiling; 3) Tomb of Ramses IX (KV9) — vibrant Book of the Dead scenes. Extra-fee highlights: Tomb of Tutankhamun (KV62, extra 600 EGP), Tomb of Seti I (KV17, extra 1,400 EGP, best-preserved tomb in the valley), and Tomb of Ramses V/VI (KV9, extra 220 EGP).
How much do Valley of the Kings tickets cost in 2026?
2026 Valley of the Kings ticket prices: Standard entry (3 tombs) 600 EGP (~$12), Tomb of Tutankhamun (KV62) extra 600 EGP, Tomb of Seti I (KV17) extra 1,400 EGP, Tomb of Ramses V/VI (KV9) extra 220 EGP, Tomb of Ay extra 220 EGP, photographic permit extra 300 EGP. Egypt Photography Tours includes ticket coordination in all Luxor tours.
What are the best photography locations on Luxor West Bank?
Top Luxor West Bank photography locations: 1) Hatshepsut Temple at Deir el-Bahari — sunrise shots with dramatic cliff backdrop; 2) Valley of the Kings exterior — sunrise over the Theban hills; 3) Valley of the Queens — Nefertari's tomb interior (extra fee, best-preserved queen's tomb); 4) Valley of the Nobles — daily life scenes and vivid colors; 5) Ramesseum and Medinet Habu — less crowded mortuary temples; 6) Colossi of Memnon — sunrise over colossal statues.
What is the best time of day to visit the Valley of the Kings?
The best time to visit the Valley of the Kings is early morning (7:00 AM opening time). The valley opens at 7:00 AM and temperatures become uncomfortably hot by mid-morning, especially in summer. Early arrival means fewer crowds, cooler temperatures, and better lighting on the Theban hills. Egypt Photography Tours starts all West Bank tours at 6:30 AM for optimal conditions.
What are the must-see sites on Luxor West Bank beyond the Valley of the Kings?
Must-see sites beyond Valley of the Kings: 1) Hatshepsut Temple at Deir el-Bahari — Egypt's most photogenic temple with dramatic cliff backdrop; 2) Valley of the Queens — includes Nefertari's tomb, the most beautiful tomb in Egypt (extra fee); 3) Valley of the Nobles — over 400 tombs of nobles with vivid daily life scenes; 4) Deir el-Medina — workers' village where tomb builders lived; 5) Ramesseum — Ramses II mortuary temple with colossal statues; 6) Medinet Habu — Ramses III mortuary temple, one of Egypt's best-preserved temples.
