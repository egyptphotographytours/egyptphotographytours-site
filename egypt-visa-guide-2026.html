Chapter 1: The 2026 Visa Price Update — What Changed?

Effective March 1, 2026, Egypt's authorities implemented a long-anticipated adjustment to the Visa on Arrival fee, while the e-Visa pricing remained untouched. Here is the official breakdown:

e-Visa

$25 USD
Single Entry
Price unchanged from 2025
Multiple-entry option: $60 USD
Processing: 48–72 hours (up to 7 days)
Pay online with credit/debit card

Visa on Arrival

$30 USD
Single Entry
Price increased from $25 (March 2026)
No multiple-entry option at the airport
Processing: immediate (queue time varies)

The Verdict on Cost

The e-Visa is $5 cheaper per person. For a family of four, choosing the e-Visa saves you $20 USD — enough for a fantastic dinner in Cairo. Add in the skipped airport queue and the peace of mind of pre-approval, and the math speaks for itself.

Chapter 2: e-Visa vs. Visa on Arrival — The Complete Breakdown

Price is just one factor. Let's dive deep into the nuances of each option so you can choose with confidence.

Option A: The Egyptian e-Visa (The Planner's Choice)

The e-Visa is Egypt's official online portal, designed for travelers who prefer to have everything sorted before they leave home.

Who is it for?

  • Planners: you like to have your ducks in a row before a trip.
  • Peak-season travelers: avoid potentially long Visa on Arrival queues during busy periods.
  • Multiple-entry needs: if you plan to leave Egypt (e.g., a side trip to Jordan) and return, the $60 multiple-entry e-Visa is your only option.
  • Peace-of-mind seekers: eliminate the "what if" of landing and facing an issue at the visa counter.

Step-by-Step e-Visa Application

  1. Visit the official portal: go to visa2egypt.gov.eg. Be wary of third-party sites that charge hidden fees.
  2. Create an account: sign up with your email address and verify it.
  3. Fill the application: select "Tourism" and choose "Single" or "Multiple" entry. Enter your details exactly as they appear on your passport.
  4. Upload documents: you'll need a scan of your passport's bio page (under 1 MB, JPG or PDF). A passport photo may also be required.
  5. Host information: this is where a tour operator helps. You'll need to enter a "Host" — for tours with Egypt Photography Tours, we provide the exact details to use.
  6. Pay and wait: pay with Visa or Mastercard. Processing typically takes 48–72 hours, but apply at least a week in advance. You'll receive your e-Visa by email as a PDF — print it and bring it with you!

Option B: Visa on Arrival (The Flexible Flyer's Choice)

The VOA is a straightforward process available at all major international airports in Egypt (Cairo, Luxor, Aswan, Hurghada and more) and at some land borders.

Who is it for?

  • Last-minute travelers: you booked your trip with less than a week to go.
  • Spontaneous types: you prefer to handle things as they come.
  • Simple itineraries: single entry, no side trips out of Egypt.

Step-by-Step Visa on Arrival Process

  1. Follow the signs: after deplaning, follow the crowd toward "Immigration" and "Banking."
  2. Find the bank counter: before you reach immigration, you'll see bank kiosks — this is where you purchase the visa sticker.
  3. Pay the fee: hand over $30 USD in clean, crisp bills per person. Exact change is crucial, as tellers often have limited change. You'll receive a sticker.
  4. Proceed to immigration: take the sticker and your passport to the officer, who will affix the sticker and stamp you in.

Critical VOA Warning

While the March 2026 fee is $30, Egypt is still using the old $25 visa stickers. To account for the increase, an additional $5 sticker is affixed next to the original one. Don't be alarmed — this is the new official procedure. Just make sure you receive both stickers for your $30.

Option C: The Sinai-Only Stamp (The Wildcard)

If you are flying directly into Sharm El Sheikh, Dahab, Nuweiba or Taba (popular Red Sea resorts), you can get a free 14-day entry stamp for the South Sinai region.

The Catch

The Sinai-only stamp strictly limits you to the South Sinai governorate. You cannot travel to Cairo, Luxor or any other part of Egypt. If you plan to see the Pyramids or the Nile Valley, you must obtain a full national visa (e-Visa or VOA).

Chapter 5: Beyond the Visa — Non-Negotiable Entry Rules

Having a visa is just one part of the equation. Make sure you meet these requirements to avoid being turned away at the gate.

Passport Validity: The Six-Month Rule

Your passport must be valid for at least six months beyond your intended date of departure from Egypt. This is strictly enforced by airlines and immigration officials. You also need at least one completely blank visa page (two is safer) for the entry stamp.

Required Documents to Carry

  • Return flight ticket: proof of onward or return travel.
  • Hotel reservations or tour itinerary: you may be asked where you're staying — your Egypt Photography Tours confirmation is perfect for this.
  • Sufficient funds: rarely asked at the airport, but good practice to be able to show means for your stay.

Health Requirements

Currently, no pandemic documentation is required. A Yellow Fever vaccination certificate is mandatory only for travelers arriving from countries with a risk of yellow fever transmission.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an Egypt visa cost in 2026?
As of March 1, 2026, the Egypt Visa on Arrival costs $30 USD. The e-Visa remains at $25 USD for a single entry and $60 USD for a multiple-entry visa.
Is the Egypt e-Visa cheaper than Visa on Arrival?
Yes, the e-Visa is cheaper at $25 USD, while the Visa on Arrival is now $30 USD following the March 2026 price increase.
Can I get an Egypt visa on arrival as a US citizen?
Yes, US citizens are eligible for the Egypt Visa on Arrival. However, it is highly recommended to bring the exact fee of $30 USD in clean, crisp bills to avoid any issues at the bank counter.
How long does it take to get an Egypt e-Visa?
The Egypt e-Visa typically takes 48 to 72 hours to be approved, though it can take up to 5–7 business days. It is best to apply at least one week before your departure.
What is the Sinai-only stamp?
The Sinai-only stamp is a free 14-day entry permit for tourists arriving at Sharm El Sheikh, Dahab, Nuweiba or Taba. It restricts you to the South Sinai region and does not permit travel to Cairo, Luxor or other parts of Egypt.
