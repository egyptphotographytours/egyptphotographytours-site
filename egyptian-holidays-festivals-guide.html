Chapter 2: Abu Simbel Sun Festival — February 22 & October 22, 2026

The Abu Simbel Sun Festival is one of Egypt's most spectacular cultural events — occurring twice yearly on February 22 and October 22, when sunlight penetrates the inner sanctuary of the Great Temple of Ramses II, illuminating the statues of Ramses II and the gods. This extraordinary phenomenon occurs at sunrise (approximately 6:30 AM) and lasts about 20 minutes, offering extraordinary photography opportunities.

Abu Simbel Sun Festival

Extraordinary Phenomenon: Twice yearly (February 22 and October 22), sunlight penetrates the inner sanctuary of the Great Temple of Ramses II, illuminating the statues of Ramses II and the gods Ptah, Amun-Ra, and Ra-Horakhty. The phenomenon occurs at sunrise (approximately 6:30 AM) and lasts about 20 minutes.

Ancient Engineering Marvel: The ancient Egyptians engineered the temple so precisely that sunlight illuminates the inner sanctuary only twice yearly — originally on Ramses II's birthday (February 22) and coronation day (October 22). When the temple was relocated in the 1960s, engineers maintained this alignment.

Photography Paradise: Sunrise illumination offers extraordinary photography opportunities. Egypt Photography Tours offers specialized photography tours with early morning access for optimal sunrise photography.

2026 Abu Simbel Sun Festival Visitor Information

  • Festival Dates: February 22 and October 22, 2026.
  • Sunrise Time: Approximately 6:30 AM — sunlight illumination lasts about 20 minutes.
  • Entry Fee: 600 EGP (~$12) — standard Abu Simbel entry fee.
  • Best Photo Time: Arrive by 6:00 AM for optimal sunrise photography and to secure good viewing position.
  • Photography Tips: Wide-angle lens (16–35mm) recommended for temple interior. Tripod recommended for sunrise photography. Arrive early to secure good viewing position — the sanctuary fills quickly.

Abu Simbel Sun Festival Photography Tips

Arrive Early: Arrive by 6:00 AM to secure good viewing position inside the sanctuary — the sanctuary fills quickly on festival days.

Camera Settings: Use wide-angle lens (16–35mm) for temple interior. Use tripod for sunrise photography. Use available light — flash photography is prohibited inside the temple.

Chapter 3: Ramadan & Eid Celebrations — March 2 - April 1, 2026

Ramadan 2026 runs from March 2 to April 1 — the Islamic holy month of fasting from dawn to sunset. Ramadan offers extraordinary cultural experiences with lantern-lit streets, festive iftar gatherings, and vibrant cultural celebrations. Eid al-Fitr (April 1-2) marks the end of Ramadan with family gatherings and festive celebrations.

Ramadan 2026 (March 2 - April 1)

Islamic Holy Month: Muslims fast from dawn to sunset during Ramadan. The month offers extraordinary cultural experiences with lantern-lit streets, festive iftar gatherings, and vibrant cultural celebrations.

Festive Atmosphere: Streets are decorated with colorful lanterns (fanoos), and iftar gatherings create festive atmosphere throughout Egypt. The month offers extraordinary photography opportunities with lantern-lit streets and festive decorations.

Photography Paradise: Lantern-lit streets, iftar gatherings, and festive decorations offer extraordinary photography opportunities. Evening photography after iftar offers vibrant cultural photography opportunities.

Ramadan 2026 Photography Opportunities

  • Lantern-Lit Streets: Streets decorated with colorful lanterns (fanoos) offer extraordinary photography opportunities — use wide-angle lens for atmospheric street photography.
  • Iftar Gatherings: Family gatherings and festive iftar meals offer cultural photography opportunities — always request permission before photographing people.
  • Festive Decorations: Colorful lanterns and festive decorations offer extraordinary photography opportunities — use available light and high ISO settings for evening photography.
  • Evening Photography: Evening photography after iftar offers vibrant cultural photography opportunities with festive atmosphere and cultural celebrations.

Ramadan Travel Tips

Dining Schedules: Many restaurants close during daylight hours during Ramadan, opening for iftar at sunset. Hotel restaurants remain open for guests.

Respectful Travel: Avoid eating, drinking, or smoking in public during daylight hours during Ramadan out of respect for those fasting.

Chapter 4: Coptic Christian Holidays — Coptic Christmas & Easter

Coptic Christians celebrate Christmas on January 7 (rather than December 25) due to different calendar calculations. Coptic Easter is celebrated on April 19, 2026. Coptic Christian holidays offer extraordinary cultural experiences with church celebrations and religious ceremonies.

Coptic Christmas (January 7, 2026)

Coptic Christmas: Coptic Christians celebrate Christmas on January 7 rather than December 25 due to different calendar calculations. The holiday offers extraordinary cultural experiences with church celebrations and religious ceremonies.

Church Celebrations: Coptic churches throughout Egypt celebrate Christmas with religious ceremonies, festive decorations, and cultural celebrations. Coptic Cairo offers extraordinary photography opportunities with ancient churches and festive decorations.

Photography Paradise: Church celebrations, religious ceremonies, and festive decorations offer extraordinary photography opportunities — use available light and high ISO settings for interior church photography.

Coptic Easter (April 19, 2026)

Coptic Easter is celebrated on April 19, 2026. The holiday offers extraordinary cultural experiences with church celebrations and religious ceremonies. Coptic churches throughout Egypt celebrate Easter with religious ceremonies and cultural celebrations.

Chapter 5: Sham El-Nessim Festival — April 20, 2026

Sham El-Nessim is one of the world's oldest festivals, dating back over 4,500 years to pharaonic times. Celebrated on the Monday after Coptic Easter (April 20, 2026), it's a national holiday celebrated by all Egyptians regardless of religion. Egyptians celebrate with picnics, eating traditional foods like salted fish (fiseekh), colored eggs, and spring onions.

Sham El-Nessim (April 20, 2026)

World's Oldest Festival: Sham El-Nessim dates back over 4,500 years to pharaonic times — one of the world's oldest festivals. Celebrated on the Monday after Coptic Easter, it's a national holiday celebrated by all Egyptians regardless of religion.

Traditional Celebrations: Egyptians celebrate with picnics, eating traditional foods like salted fish (fiseekh), colored eggs, and spring onions. The festival celebrates spring and the renewal of life.

Photography Paradise: Spring celebrations, traditional foods, family picnics, and festive atmosphere offer extraordinary photography opportunities.

Chapter 8: Practical Holiday Travel Tips

Holiday travel in Egypt offers unique cultural experiences but requires careful planning. This chapter provides practical travel tips for holiday travel in Egypt.

Holiday Travel Tips

Book Early: Book accommodations and tours well in advance during major holidays — prices increase and availability decreases during peak holiday periods.

Expect Crowds: Expect higher crowds during major holidays like Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, and national holidays.

Business Closures: Some businesses close during major holidays. Plan accordingly and book accommodations and tours in advance.

Ramadan Travel: During Ramadan, many restaurants close during daylight hours. Hotel restaurants remain open for guests. Respectful travel is important — avoid eating, drinking, or smoking in public during daylight hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the Abu Simbel Sun Festival in 2026?
The Abu Simbel Sun Festival occurs twice yearly: February 22 and October 22, 2026. On these dates, sunlight penetrates the inner sanctuary of the Great Temple, illuminating the statues of Ramses II and the gods. The phenomenon occurs at sunrise (approximately 6:30 AM) and lasts about 20 minutes. Egypt Photography Tours offers specialized photography tours with early morning access.
What are the major Islamic holidays in Egypt in 2026?
Major Islamic holidays in Egypt 2026: 1) Ramadan (March 2 - April 1): Month of fasting; 2) Eid al-Fitr (April 1-2): Celebration marking end of Ramadan; 3) Eid al-Adha (approximately June 6-9): Festival of Sacrifice; 4) Mawlid al-Nabi (approximately September 26): Prophet Muhammad's birthday. Islamic holidays follow lunar calendar and shift approximately 11 days earlier each year.
When is Coptic Christmas and Easter in Egypt in 2026?
Coptic Christian holidays in Egypt 2026: 1) Coptic Christmas: January 7, 2026 (celebrated by Coptic Orthodox Church); 2) Coptic Easter: April 19, 2026 (Coptic Orthodox Easter). Coptic Christians celebrate Christmas on January 7 rather than December 25 due to different calendar calculations.
What is Sham El-Nessim and when is it celebrated in 2026?
Sham El-Nessim is an ancient Egyptian spring festival celebrated on the Monday after Coptic Easter (April 20, 2026). It's one of the world's oldest festivals, dating back over 4,500 years to pharaonic times. Egyptians celebrate with picnics, eating traditional foods like salted fish (fiseekh), colored eggs, and spring onions. It's a national holiday celebrated by all Egyptians regardless of religion.
What photography opportunities exist during Egyptian holidays?
Top Egyptian holiday photography opportunities: 1) Abu Simbel Sun Festival: Sunrise illumination of Ramses II statues; 2) Ramadan: Lantern-lit streets, iftar gatherings, festive decorations; 3) Eid celebrations: Family gatherings, traditional clothing, festive atmosphere; 4) Sham El-Nessim: Spring celebrations, traditional foods, family picnics; 5) Coptic Christmas: Church celebrations, religious ceremonies.
Should I avoid traveling to Egypt during major holidays?
Not necessarily. Major holidays offer unique cultural experiences and photography opportunities. However, expect: 1) Higher prices and crowds during Eid holidays; 2) Some businesses closed during major holidays; 3) Ramadan offers unique cultural experiences but different dining schedules; 4) Book accommodations and tours well in advance during peak holiday periods.
